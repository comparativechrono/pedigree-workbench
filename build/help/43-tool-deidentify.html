De-identify

This tool makes a copy of a GA4GH or Phenopackets file for sharing. Everyone gets a new id, which is used consistently everywhere in the file. Dates of birth and other ids are taken out, and you can also hide who made the file. You can keep the key from the old ids to the new ones in a separate file.

The form

New ids
Choose IND-0001, IND-0002 … to number the people in the order they come in the file. With codes made from the old ids (such as IND-3f9a1c2b7d40), a person gets the same code in every file, as long as you use the same secret. This means you can follow the same person across files without their id. Or you can keep the ids as they are.
Secret for the codes
Use any words you like, and keep them private. Without a secret, anyone who has the old ids could make the codes again, and so match each code to its old id.
Keep dates of birth, Ages
Dates of birth are taken out unless you choose to keep them. You can put the age at the last encounter into bands of 5 or 10 years. For example, in 5-year bands, 47 becomes 45. Ages of onset aren't changed.
Keep other ids
A person's other ids (alternateIds, such as a hospital number) are taken out unless you choose to keep them.
Hide who made the file, and when
The metaData has to say who made the file and when. So instead of the original createdBy and created, it says phenotk deidentify and the time of the de-identification. The record of updates and submittedBy are taken out.
Save the key as
This is a JSON file of old id → new id. Keep it apart from the shared file, because with it, anyone can follow the new ids back to the old ones.

What changes in the file

  • The ids change everywhere: in each Phenopacket and its subject, the relationships, the index patient, a Family's pedigree, biosamples, genomic interpretations and files.
  • The ids in a GA4GH Pedigree's narrative change too. For example, proband IV-3 becomes proband IND-0014. The narrative is free text, so read it before you share the file. The tool reminds you to. If some of the ids are bare numbers, the tool can't tell them from other numbers in the text, so it leaves the narrative unchanged and warns you.
  • Nothing else changes. Terms, sexes, relationships and the shape of the family stay as they were.
De-identified is not anonymous

De-identifying removes what the file says directly. But a family can still be recognised from what's left, such as a rare condition, a large or unusual family, people's ages or where it came from. Someone who knows the family is especially likely to recognise it. It's for you to judge whether a file can be shared, and with whom, under your institution's rules and the law on patient data.

In the terminal

phenotk deidentify /data/examples/okafor_ga4gh-pedigree.json -o ~/results/okafor.deidentified.json --keyfile ~/results/okafor.key.json
phenotk view ~/results/okafor.deidentified.json
Option
--remap-ids seq|hash, --keep-ids, --salt TEXTthe new ids
--keep-dob, --bin-age 5y|10y, --keep-names, --drop-provenancewhat is kept
--keyfile FILEwhere the key goes

In Python

import json
import phenopacket_toolkit as ptk
from phenopacket_toolkit.ops import deidentify

doc = ptk.read("/data/examples/okafor_ga4gh-pedigree.json")
res = deidentify(doc, remap_ids="seq")
ptk.write(res.document, "/home/user/results/okafor.deidentified.json")
json.dump(res.crosswalk, open("/home/user/results/okafor.key.json", "w"), indent=2)
for w in res.warnings:
    print("warning:", w)
