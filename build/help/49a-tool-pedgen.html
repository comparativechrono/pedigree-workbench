Simulate a family

PedGen makes up a family with an autosomal dominant or recessive condition, by Mendelian inheritance from an affected founder, and writes it as a PED file. Use it for teaching, or to try PedNet on families whose mode of inheritance you know.

How it works

The founder has the condition. In each generation, every child gets one of each parent's two copies of the gene, at random, and each couple has between one child and the most you allow. The first child of a couple always has a partner, and their brothers and sisters each have one with the chance you give, so the family grows down the generations. Partners come from a population in which the condition's allele has the frequency you give, and some come with their own parents, and theirs. Who is affected follows from the genotypes: one copy of the allele is enough for a dominant condition, and a recessive one needs two. As everyone's genotype is known, PedGen can write them too, as a candidate variant for Rank candidate variants.

The form

Mode of inheritance, Generations, The most children a couple has
The family's condition and shape.
Seed
A whole number. With the same seed and settings, PedGen makes the same family, here, in the terminal and in Python. Leave it empty to get a new family every time.
Family id
It names the PED file: FAM1.ped, unless you change it.
Chance that a child has a partner, and children; Chance that a partner's parents are added
0.6 and 0.25, unless you change them.
Frequency of the allele among partners
0.1, unless you change it. That is a common allele: about one partner in five carries it, and one in a hundred has two copies, so a recessive condition turns up again in later generations far more often than a rare one would. For a rare condition, give 0.01 or less. Partners never carry the allele keeps it to the founder and their descendants.
Save everyone's genotypes too
A table, FAM1-genotypes.tsv, of the number of copies of the allele that each person has, as a variant called causal, for Rank candidate variants.

What PedNet makes of it

PedNet takes a condition to be rare, and its recessive model gives the allele a frequency of 1 in 1,000. In a recessive family that PedGen makes with its frequency of 0.1, several of the partners who marry in may be carriers, which PedNet's model finds very unlikely, so PedNet may favour another mode, or be uncertain. Given PedGen's frequency, in Python, PedNet weighs the family up as PedGen made it. Dominant families are clearer, though one of three generations is often too small for a confident answer.

In the terminal

pedgen --mode AD --generations 4 --seed 4 -d ~/results --genotypes ~/results/FAM1-genotypes.tsv
pednet mode ~/results/FAM1.ped
pednet rank ~/results/FAM1.ped ~/results/FAM1-genotypes.tsv
ped2studio ~/results/FAM1.ped ~/pedigrees/simulated.studio.json
Option
-m, --mode AD|ARthe mode of inheritance (default: AD)
-g, --generations N; -c, --max-children Nthe number of generations (3), and the most children a couple can have (3)
-s, --seed Nmakes the family reproducible
-f, --family ID; -d, --output-dir FOLDERthe family id, which names the file (FAM1), and where to write it
--partners CHANCE, --ancestors CHANCEthe chance that a child has a partner (0.6), and that a partner's parents are added (0.25)
--allele-frequency F, --unaffected-partnersthe allele's frequency among partners (0.1), or none at all
--genotypes FILEalso write everyone's genotypes, as a table

In Python

import pednet
from PedGen import pedigree_generator, gen_PED_export

df = pedigree_generator(max_children=3, FamilyID="SIM1", mode="AR", generation_count=4, seed=34)
print(df.head())
pedigree = pednet.pedigree_from_rows(df)
print(pednet.infer_mode(pedigree).call, "for a rare allele;",
      pednet.infer_mode(pedigree, allele_frequency={"AR": 0.1}).call, "at PedGen's frequency")
print("wrote", gen_PED_export(df, "/home/user/results"))

PedGen's plot_pedigree_tree draws a family with matplotlib, but Pedigree Studio draws it better: draw the PED file with PED or MPED → drawing.

The first time

PedGen needs NumPy and pandas, which Python downloads the first time you use it. That is about 9 MB, or less if PedNet has already run, and your browser keeps them for next time.
