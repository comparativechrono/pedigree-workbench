Simulate a family

PedGen makes up a family with an autosomal dominant or recessive condition, by Mendelian inheritance from an affected founder, and writes it as a PED file. Use it for teaching, or to try PedNet on families whose mode of inheritance you know.

How it works

The founder has the condition. In each generation, every child gets one of each parent's two copies of the gene, at random, and each couple has between one child and the most you allow. In every generation but the last, the first child of a couple always has a partner and children, and their brothers and sisters each have them with the chance you give, so that the family has as many generations as you ask for. Partners come from a population in which the condition's allele has the frequency you give, a rare one unless you change it, and some come with their own parents, and theirs. Who is affected follows from the genotypes: one copy of the allele is enough for a dominant condition, and a recessive one needs two. As everyone's genotype is known, PedGen can write them too, as a candidate variant for Rank candidate variants.

A rare recessive condition

With a rare allele, someone who marries into the family almost never carries it, so a recessive condition seldom turns up again in the family unless relatives marry. That is so of rare recessive diseases too, which are commoner in the children of cousins. So for a recessive condition, the form lets cousins marry whenever they can, and makes families until one has at least two people affected, unless you change these. Cousins are first found among the founder's grandchildren, in the third generation, so a recessive family needs four generations or more. At the default frequency, only about one four-generation family in seventy has a second person affected, so PedGen makes about seventy families, on average, before it keeps one, which takes a few seconds.

The form

Mode of inheritance, Generations, The most children a couple has
The family's condition and shape: 3 generations, or 4 for a recessive condition, unless you change it.
Seed
A whole number. With the same seed and settings, PedGen makes the same family, here, in the terminal and in Python. Leave it empty to get a new family every time.
Family id
It names the PED file: FAM1.ped, unless you change it.
Chance that a child has a partner, and children; Chance that a partner's parents are added
0.6 and 0.25, unless you change them.
Frequency of the allele among partners
0.001, unless you change it: a rare allele, as a rare disease's is. At 0.1, about one partner in five would carry it, and in a dominant family be affected. Partners never carry the allele gives the partners who marry in no copy of it, but their parents and grandparents, if they're added, can still carry it. A frequency of 0 keeps the allele to the founder and their descendants.
Chance that someone marries a cousin
1 for a recessive condition and 0 for a dominant one, unless you change it: the chance that someone in the family who has a partner marries a cousin rather than someone from outside the family, when a cousin has no partner yet. A marriage between cousins makes a loop in the pedigree, which The pedigree as a graph shows.
At least this many affected
2 for a recessive condition and 1 for a dominant one, unless you change it. PedGen makes families until one has as many affected people, as families come to notice through their affected members. If none of 1,000 has them, it stops and says what to change.
Save everyone's genotypes too
A table, FAM1-genotypes.tsv, of the number of copies of the allele that each person has, as a variant called causal, for Rank candidate variants.

What PedNet makes of it

PedNet takes a condition to be rare too: for autosomal recessive inheritance, it gives the allele a frequency of 1 in 1,000, as PedGen does. Of 30 recessive families of four generations made as the form makes them, PedNet called 16 autosomal recessive, and put that mode first in 25. Of 30 of five generations, it called 21 autosomal recessive. In a family where an affected person marries a carrier, half their children can be affected, which looks dominant (pseudodominance), and then PedNet may favour autosomal dominant inheritance. Dominant families are clearer, but in one family PedNet often can't tell autosomal from X-linked dominant inheritance unless a father passes the condition to a son.

In the terminal

pedgen --mode AD --generations 4 --seed 4 -d ~/results --genotypes ~/results/FAM1-genotypes.tsv
pednet mode ~/results/FAM1.ped
pednet rank ~/results/FAM1.ped ~/results/FAM1-genotypes.tsv
pedgen --mode AR --generations 4 --cousins 1 --min-affected 2 --seed 12 --family FAM2 -d ~/results
pednet graph ~/results/FAM2.ped
ped2studio ~/results/FAM2.ped ~/pedigrees/simulated.studio.json

In the terminal, the defaults are the same for both modes: 3 generations, no cousins marrying, and one person affected. Give the form's for a recessive condition as above.

Option
-m, --mode AD|ARthe mode of inheritance (default: AD)
-g, --generations N; -c, --max-children Nthe number of generations (3), and the most children a couple can have (3)
-s, --seed Nmakes the family reproducible
-f, --family ID; -d, --output-dir FOLDERthe family id, which names the file (FAM1), and where to write it
--partners CHANCE, --ancestors CHANCEthe chance that a child has a partner (0.6), and that a partner's parents are added (0.25)
--allele-frequency F, --unaffected-partnersthe allele's frequency among partners and their ancestors (0.001); with --unaffected-partners, the partners themselves never carry it
--cousins CHANCEthe chance that someone marries a cousin, when one has no partner yet (0)
--min-affected Nmake families until one has at least N people affected (1)
--genotypes FILEalso write everyone's genotypes, as a table

In Python

import pednet
from PedGen import pedigree_generator, gen_PED_export

# a rare recessive condition, which turns up again where cousins marry
df = pedigree_generator(max_children=3, FamilyID="SIM2", mode="AR", generation_count=4,
                        CousinLikelihood=1.0, min_affected=2, seed=12)
pedigree = pednet.pedigree_from_rows(df)
print(len(df), "people;", len(pednet.loops(pedigree)), "loop, where cousins married")
print(pednet.infer_mode(pedigree))
print("wrote", gen_PED_export(df, "/home/user/results"))

PedGen's plot_pedigree_tree draws a family with matplotlib. In a notebook, put import matplotlib.pyplot as plt in the same cell, so that Python loads matplotlib before PedGen needs it. Pedigree Studio draws a family better, though: draw the PED file with PED or MPED → drawing.

The first time

PedGen needs NumPy and pandas, which Python downloads the first time you use it. That is about 9 MB, or less if PedNet has already run, and your browser keeps them for next time.
