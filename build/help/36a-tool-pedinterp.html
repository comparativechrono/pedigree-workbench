Picture → PED file

PedInterp reads a picture of a pedigree, such as one in a paper, a clinic letter or a scan, and writes it as a PED file. It finds the symbols and the lines between them, and so who is male or female, who is affected, and who are partners, parents and children.

The form

The picture
A PNG or JPEG file, or a GIF, BMP, TIFF or WebP picture. From my computer… adds one of your own, in ~/pictures.
Family id
The family's id in the PED file, which also names the file. It starts as the picture's name.
Save in
The folder for the PED file: ~/results, unless you choose another.
Enlarge the picture by
Leave it empty, as it is, and PedInterp chooses: it finds the symbols in a copy of the picture 2,000 pixels across, and enlarges the picture so that they are about 200 pixels across, which reads best. A line under the table says how much it enlarged it: 5.6 times for the Lynch family's picture, which is about 1,000 pixels across, and more for a smaller picture, less for a larger one or a scan. There is some room either side: the Lynch picture reads correctly at anything from 4 to 12 times, but not at 3 or 15. If PedInterp warns that a line may have been missed, give a number a little larger or smaller than the one it chose: smaller, if the lines are very thick. It warns, too, if the picture is too small to enlarge enough, or a page too large, which reads better cut down to the pedigree.

What it reads

PedInterp reads pedigrees drawn in the usual way, in black on white: squares, circles and diamonds, filled in for the people who are affected, with each generation in a row of its own. It follows the lines between partners, down from partners to their children, and over a family of brothers and sisters. A slash through a symbol, for someone who has died, doesn't usually stop it reading the symbol, though a slash at an odd angle can make it mistake the person's sex. The father is the male partner and the mother the female one, whichever side each is drawn on.

Some things aren't read yet: symbols that are half filled or shaded (as they often are for carriers), lines that slope (as they do for twins), and dashed lines. A PED file has no place for deaths, twins or adoptions anyway. So compare the PED file with the picture before you use it. The quickest way is to draw it, with PED or MPED → drawing.

Who is who

On a computer of your own, PedInterp reads the labels under the symbols with the Tesseract program. A browser can't run Tesseract, so here the people are numbered by generation instead: from the top, and from the left in each generation, I-1, I-2, II-1 and so on. Pedigree Studio numbers people in the same way. The picture of the Lynch family was drawn where the Studio draws the family, so its numbers are the same as those in the Lynch PED file, and the PED file that PedInterp writes is the same as that one, apart from the family's id.

In the terminal

pedinterp /data/examples/lynch.png --labels none -d ~/results
diff <(cut -f2- ~/results/lynch.ped) <(cut -f2- /data/examples/lynch.ped) && echo "the same people, parents, sexes and status"
Option
-f, --family IDthe family id, which names the PED file (default: the picture's name)
-d, --output-dir FOLDERwhere to write the PED file (default: the folder you're in)
--upscale FACTORhow many times to enlarge the picture (by default, as many as its symbols need: the table says how many)
--labels tesseract|noneread the labels with Tesseract, or number the people. Here the people are numbered either way, but with none there's no warning that Tesseract isn't there.
-q, --quietdon't print the table

In Python

from PedInterp import pedigree_processing, PED_export

df = pedigree_processing("LYNCH", image_path="/data/examples/lynch.png", ocr=None)
print(df.to_string(index=False))
print("wrote", PED_export(df, "/home/user/results"))

ocr=None numbers the people. ocr can also be a function that reads the labels with other OCR, such as tesseract.js in a web page: PedInterp's README explains how.

The first time

PedInterp needs NumPy, pandas, OpenCV and Pillow, which Python downloads the first time you use it. That is about 21 MB, or less if PedNet or PedGen has already run, and your browser keeps them for next time.
