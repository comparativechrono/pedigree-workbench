Picture → PED file

PedInterp reads a picture of a pedigree, such as one in a paper, a clinic letter or a scan, or one sketched by hand, and writes it as a PED file, and as a GA4GH Pedigree if you ask. It finds the symbols and the lines between them, and so who is male or female, who is affected, who are partners, parents and children, and which children are twins.

The form

The picture
A PNG or JPEG file, or a GIF, BMP, TIFF or WebP picture. From my computer… adds one of your own, in ~/pictures.
Drawn by hand
Tick it for a pedigree sketched by hand, in whole or in part: with Pedigree Studio's Draw tool, say, or begun with the Studio's symbols and finished with the Draw tool, or drawn on paper and scanned. PedInterp then allows for symbols of unequal sizes, outlines that don't quite close or that run on past where they began, symbols filled in by scribbling, and lines that wobble and stop short of what they join or run a little past it. Leave it unticked for a picture drawn with software, such as one Pedigree Studio saves, which reads more surely so. If PedInterp warns that lines may have been missed in a picture read without it, and says the pedigree may be drawn by hand, try again with it ticked.
Family id
The family's id in the PED file, which also names the file. It starts as the picture's name.
Save in
The folder for the PED file: ~/results, unless you choose another.
Enlarge the picture by
Leave it empty, as it is, and PedInterp chooses: it finds the symbols in a copy of the picture 2,000 pixels across, and enlarges the picture so that they are about 200 pixels across, which reads best (100 for a sketch, about the size the Studio draws them at). A line under the table says how much it enlarged it: 5.6 times for the Lynch family's picture, which is about 1,000 pixels across, and more for a smaller picture, less for a larger one or a scan. There is some room either side: the Lynch picture reads correctly at anything from 4 to 12 times, but not at 3 or 15. If PedInterp warns that a line may have been missed, give a number a little larger or smaller than the one it chose: smaller, if the lines are very thick. It warns, too, if the picture is too small to enlarge enough, or a page too large, which reads better cut down to the pedigree.
Write a GA4GH Pedigree too
The family as a GA4GH Pedigree, FAMILY_ga4gh-pedigree.json, beside the PED file, which ga4gh2studio, the phenopacket toolkit, PedNet and the Risk tab read. Give the condition as an ontology term and its name, such as MONDO:0007254 and breast cancer, or leave them empty for MONDO:0000001, disease. The affected people have the condition, and the unaffected have it marked as looked for and not found. The file notes which picture the family was read from, and how much it was enlarged.

What it reads

PedInterp reads pedigrees drawn in the usual way, dark on white: squares, circles and diamonds, filled in for the people who are affected, with each generation in a row of its own. It follows the lines between partners, down from partners to their children, and over a family of brothers and sisters. A slash through a symbol, for someone who has died, doesn't usually stop it reading the symbol, though a slash at an odd angle can make it mistake the person's sex. The father is the male partner and the mother the female one, whichever side each is drawn on. Pedigree Studio's numbers over the symbols, in grey, are taken out before the lines are read, even where they touch a line.

Twins are two children whose lines come down from one point, and identical twins have a bar across between the two lines. A PED file has no place for twins, so they are listed under the table (twins: IV-3 and IV-4 (identical)), and a GA4GH Pedigree records them, as Pedigree Studio's files do. The Okafor family has identical twins, IV-3 and IV-4.

With Drawn by hand, PedInterp reads pedigrees sketched roughly, as testers in a clinic sketch them with the Studio's Draw tool: in any colour, with symbols of unequal sizes, filled in with scribbles, and lines that wobble. Of 48 sketches made with the Draw tool by a program that imitates a hand, for testing, it read 40 exactly right, 445 of their 459 people right, and 20 of their 23 pairs of twins; of 24 pictures drawn with the Studio's symbols, it read every person and every pair of twins right. Twins are what it most often gets wrong in a sketch, as the bar of identical twins is short and drawn close to the other lines, and a line drawn close beside a symbol filled in with scribbles can be taken for part of it. The Okafor sketch here is one it reads right.

Some things aren't read yet: symbols that are half filled or shaded (as they often are for carriers), and dashed lines. A PED file has no place for deaths, twins or adoptions anyway. So compare the PED file with the picture before you use it, and a sketch most of all. The quickest way is to draw it: under the result, Draw it makes a drawing from the PED file (PED or MPED → drawing), or from the GA4GH Pedigree if you wrote one, which keeps the twins (GA4GH or Phenopackets → drawing).

Who is who

On a computer of your own, PedInterp reads the labels under the symbols with the Tesseract program. A browser can't run Tesseract, so here the people are numbered by generation instead: from the top, and from the left in each generation, I-1, I-2, II-1 and so on. Pedigree Studio numbers people in the same way. The picture of the Lynch family was drawn where the Studio draws the family, so its numbers are the same as those in the Lynch PED file, and the PED file that PedInterp writes is the same as that one, apart from the family's id. The two pictures of the Okafor family read as the people, parents and sexes of okafor.mped, with those who have either of its conditions affected, as a picture shows who is affected but not by what.

In the terminal

pedinterp /data/examples/lynch.png --labels none -d ~/results
diff <(cut -f2- ~/results/lynch.ped) <(cut -f2- /data/examples/lynch.ped) && echo "the same people, parents, sexes and status"
pedinterp /data/examples/okafor-sketch.png --hand-drawn -f OKAFOR -d ~/results --ga4gh

The last reads the Okafor sketch, prints its twins under the table, and writes the GA4GH Pedigree as well as the PED file.

Option
-f, --family IDthe family id, which names the PED file (default: the picture's name)
-d, --output-dir FOLDERwhere to write the PED file (default: the folder you're in)
--hand-drawnthe pedigree is sketched by hand, in whole or in part (Drawn by hand)
--upscale FACTORhow many times to enlarge the picture (by default, as many as its symbols need: the table says how many)
--ga4gh, --condition ID LABELalso write the family as a GA4GH Pedigree, FAMILY_ga4gh-pedigree.json, with the condition as an ontology term and its name (default: MONDO:0000001 disease)
--labels tesseract|noneread the labels with Tesseract, or number the people (by default, Tesseract, but not for a sketch, whose labels are written by hand). Here the people are numbered either way, but with none there's no warning that Tesseract isn't there.
-q, --quietdon't print the table

In Python

from PedInterp import pedigree_processing, PED_export

df = pedigree_processing("LYNCH", image_path="/data/examples/lynch.png", ocr=None)
print(df.to_string(index=False))
print("wrote", PED_export(df, "/home/user/results"))

sketch = pedigree_processing("OKAFOR", image_path="/data/examples/okafor-sketch.png", ocr=None, hand_drawn=True)
print(sketch.attrs["twins"])   # [["IV-3", "IV-4", "MZ"]]: MZ for identical twins, DZ for twins who aren't

ocr=None numbers the people, and hand_drawn=True reads a sketch. ocr can also be a function that reads the labels with other OCR, such as tesseract.js in a web page: PedInterp's README explains how.

The first time

PedInterp needs NumPy, pandas, OpenCV and Pillow, which Python downloads the first time you use it. That is about 21 MB, or less if PedNet or PedGen has already run, and your browser keeps them for next time.
