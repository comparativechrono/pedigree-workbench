The Studio tab

The Studio tab is Pedigree Studio, our program for drawing pedigrees in the standard nomenclature, running inside the workbench. The bar above it connects the Studio to your files. It shows which file the drawing you're working on belongs to, saves the drawing there and takes it to the Tools tab.

The bar

What it does
Drawing and the file's nameShows the file the drawing belongs to, or not saved yet. Click it to see your other drawings, with the most recent first. Next to it you'll see saved or unsaved changes.
NewStarts an empty drawing. If the drawing you're working on has unsaved changes, you're asked first.
Open…Opens one of your drawings, one of the examples, or a drawing from your computer.
Save (Ctrl+S)Saves the drawing in its file. If the drawing doesn't have a file yet, you're asked for a name.
Save as…Saves the drawing as a new file in ~/pedigrees, unless you choose another folder in your home folder. The file's name ends in .studio.json.
TidyRuns Pedigree Studio's Tidy, which lays out the whole pedigree again. Undo in the Studio puts your own layout back.
Convert or check…Saves the drawing, then shows you everything you can do with it in the Tools tab.
⋯Lets you download the drawing, show its file in the Files tab, or get help.

Opening drawings

The examples can't be changed, so an example opens as a new drawing that hasn't been saved yet. Change it as much as you like, then use Save as… to keep your version. You might also have a drawing from somewhere else, saved from Pedigree Studio's own File menu or sent to you by someone. Open it with Open… → Open a drawing from my computer…. The workbench recognises a Pedigree Studio file by what's in it, whatever the file is called.

To open a drawing that a tool has just made, press Open the drawing in Studio in the tool's result, or type studio FILE in the terminal.

Where the drawing is kept

A saved drawing is one of your files, and like the others it's kept in this browser (Your files). Pedigree Studio also keeps the drawing you're working on, just as you left it. So if you close the page without saving, the drawing is still there when you come back. If it has a file, you'll see unsaved changes next to the file's name until you save it. If it has never been saved, the bar says not saved yet.

Pedigree Studio's own File menu still works just as it does when you use Pedigree Studio by itself. It saves the drawing as a file on your computer, opens a drawing from your computer, and exports pictures as PNG, or as SVG for documents and posters. To keep a drawing among your files, use Save on the bar.

Drawing a pedigree

Pedigree Studio's own help, under its ? button, explains how to draw, covering the symbols, the toolbar and the panels. In short:

  1. Add the first person with Male, Female or Unknown on the toolbar. This is usually the person the pedigree is for, and the one you mark as the proband in the panel.
  2. Build out from them with Add a relative. With someone selected, it adds a partner, a child, parents, or a brother or sister, already joined up.
  3. Join people yourself with Partners, Child and Twins.
  4. Describe each person in the panel. You can add notes under the symbol, mark someone as deceased and add shading for a condition. You can also put a mark inside the symbol for a carrier, a pregnancy, a donor (D) or a gestational carrier (G, which the 2025 correction to the nomenclature uses in place of S; drawings made before still show S, and the tools read it the same way).
  5. Click a line to mark a couple as consanguineous or separated, or to make a line of descent dashed to show an adoption.
  6. Tidy lays it all out again.

What the tools read from a drawing

  • The colour key names the conditions. Give each condition its own colour (or a half, a quarter or a pattern), and name it in the key with the Key button. A colour that the key doesn't name isn't counted as a condition. An id in brackets after the name, as in Breast cancer [MONDO:0007254], gives the condition its term (Ontology terms and terms files). A long name widens the key to fit it on one line, unless you choose Wrap the text under Long entries in the key in the Studio's File menu; then drag the key's right edge to set its width.
  • The proband becomes the index patient in the GA4GH files.
  • The notes under the symbols stay in the drawing. The GA4GH and PED files name people by their pedigree numbers, unless you choose otherwise.
  • Carriers, pregnancies, losses, donors and gestational carriers are written in the ways the standard allows. Losses and pregnancies are written as people, and donors and gestational carriers by their KIN relationships, as are twins, including twins whose zygosity isn't known. Anything that has no place of its own, such as a carrier or a consultand, goes in the GA4GH Pedigree's narrative.
  • A symbol that stands for several people, shown by a number inside it, is written as one person, and the tool tells you so under the result.
