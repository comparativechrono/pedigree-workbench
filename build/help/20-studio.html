The Studio tab

The Studio tab is Pedigree Studio, our program for drawing pedigrees in the standard nomenclature, inside the workbench. The bar above it joins it to your files: it says which file the drawing on show belongs to, saves it there, and takes it to the Tools tab.

Opening drawings

The examples cannot be changed, so one opens as a drawing that is not in a file of yours yet: change it as you like, and Save as… keeps your version. A drawing from somewhere else – saved from Pedigree Studio's own File menu, or sent to you – can be opened with Open… → Open a drawing from my computer…; the workbench recognises a Pedigree Studio file by what is in it, whatever it is called.

A drawing that a tool has just made opens from the tool's result (Open the drawing in Studio), and from the terminal with studio FILE.

Where the drawing is kept

A saved drawing is a file among your files, kept in this browser like the others (Your files). Pedigree Studio also keeps the drawing on show, as it is, so if you close the page without saving, it is still there when you come back – with changes not saved beside its name, until you save it.

Pedigree Studio's own File menu still works as it does on its own: it saves the drawing to your computer as a file, opens one from your computer, and exports pictures (PNG, or SVG for documents and posters). To keep a drawing among your files, use the bar's Save.

Drawing a pedigree

Pedigree Studio's own help, its ? button, explains the drawing: the symbols, the toolbar and the panels. In short:

  1. Add the first person with Male, Female or Unknown on the toolbar – usually the person the pedigree is for, marked as the proband in the panel.
  2. Build out from them: with someone selected, Add a relative adds a partner, child, parents or brother or sister, already joined up.
  3. Join people yourself with Partners, Child and Twins.
  4. Describe each person in the panel: notes under the symbol, deceased, shading for a condition, and a mark inside the symbol for a carrier, a pregnancy, a donor or a surrogate.
  5. Click a line to mark a couple consanguineous or separated, or to dash a line of descent for adoption.
  6. Tidy lays it all out afresh.

What the tools read from a drawing

  • The colour key names the conditions. Give each condition its own colour (or a half, a quarter or a pattern), and name it in the key (the Key button). A colour the key does not name is not counted as a condition. An id in brackets after the name – Breast cancer [MONDO:0007254] – gives the condition its term (Ontology terms).
  • The proband becomes the index patient of the GA4GH files.
  • The notes under the symbols stay in the drawing. The GA4GH files name people by their pedigree numbers unless you choose otherwise; Drawing → PED uses the notes as the people's ids.
  • Carriers, pregnancies, losses, donors and surrogates are written as the standard allows: losses and pregnancies as people, donors and surrogates by their KIN relationships, and what has no place of its own, such as a carrier, in the GA4GH Pedigree's narrative.
  • A symbol that stands for several people (a number inside it) is written as one person, with a note.
