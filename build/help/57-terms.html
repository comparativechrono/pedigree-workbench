Ontology terms and terms files

An ontology is a curated list of terms, each with an id, a label, a definition and its place among the others. GA4GH files name conditions by such terms, so that software everywhere reads breast cancer the same way, whatever it was called in the clinic.

The two ontologies

The Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO)
Signs and symptoms – phenotypes – such as HP:0000365 Hearing impairment. In a Phenopacket they are phenotypicFeatures, and can be marked excluded (looked for and not found).
The Mondo Disease Ontology
Diseases, such as MONDO:0007254 breast cancer, brought together from other disease vocabularies (OMIM, Orphanet, ICD and others). In a Phenopacket they are diseases.

Use a Mondo term when there is a diagnosis, and HPO terms for what was seen, with or without one.

Ids and releases

  • An id is a CURIE: a prefix, a colon and a number – HP:0000365, MONDO:0007254. (HP_0000365, as some web pages write it, is the same term; the workbench writes the colon.)
  • The ontologies are released every month or so; a term may change its label, and now and then is made obsolete and replaced. So each GA4GH file records the release its terms came from, in its metaData.

Where a condition gets its term

  1. In the drawing's colour key: an id in brackets after the name – Condition X [MONDO:0009061]. It must be an ontology's id: Hypertension (BP:160/95) is read as a name. The drawing then carries its term wherever it goes. Match conditions to terms can write the ids into a copy of the drawing.
  2. In a terms file, given to Drawing → GA4GH or to Fill in missing terms.

The terms file

A JSON file that maps each condition's name, as the colour key has it, to its term. Match conditions to terms writes it, with each term's label and release:

{
  "Breast cancer": {"id": "MONDO:0007254", "label": "breast cancer", "version": "2026-10-06"},
  "Hearing loss from birth": {"id": "HP:0000365", "label": "Hearing impairment", "version": "2026-09-01"}
}
  • An id alone will do: "Ovarian cancer": "MONDO:0008170". Without a label, the condition's own name is used; without a release, it is recorded as unknown.
  • The names are matched without regard to case, so one terms file can serve many drawings of a project.
  • The log that Fill in missing terms writes (….log.json) can be used as a terms file too.

Looking terms up

The HPO and Mondo can be searched on their own sites, on EBI's Ontology Lookup Service, or here with Match conditions to terms – online through EBI, or offline in copies of the ontologies (hp.obo, mondo.obo). Give the conditions ontology terms has advice on choosing.
