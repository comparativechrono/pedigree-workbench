Pedigree Studio

  1. Add the first person with Male, Female or Unknown on the toolbar. This is usually the person the pedigree is for, so switch on Proband in the panel that opens.
  2. Build out from them. With someone selected, Add a relative puts in a partner, son, daughter, parents, brother or sister, already joined up, and lays the pedigree out again to make room. Older and Younger move a person, with their family, along their row of brothers and sisters, which runs from the oldest on the left.
  3. Join people yourself with Partners, Child and Twins on the toolbar. Partners: click two people. Child: click a relationship line, then the child. Twins: choose identical or non-identical in the panel, then click two children of the same couple.
  4. Describe each person in the panel: notes under the symbol (name, age, diagnoses and the age at diagnosis), deceased, shading for anyone affected, and a mark inside the symbol for carriers, pregnancies, donors, surrogates or a number of people.
  5. Click a line to change it. A relationship line can be marked consanguineous or separated, and you can add the couple's children from there, including a pregnancy loss, no children or infertility. A line of descent can be dashed for adoption.
  6. Tidy lays the whole pedigree out again: generations in rows, couples side by side, brothers and sisters together under their parents in the order you drew them, and as few crossing lines as possible. Undo puts your own layout back.
  7. Save and export from File: a picture (PNG, or SVG for documents and posters) or a pedigree file (.json) that you can open again later. The pedigree you are working on is also kept in this browser until you clear it.

The toolbar

Moving around

Drag a person to move them. Dragging moves their whole connected family unless Move one person at a time is switched on under File. Drag the background to move the view, scroll or pinch to zoom, and use the button at the bottom right to fit everything in.

Colours and the key

Use a different shading colour, or halves and quarters, for each condition. Key adds a box listing the colours in use; type what each one means into it. The key is included when you export.

These are the symbols of the standard pedigree nomenclature (Bennett et al., 2008 and 2022), drawn as Pedigree Studio draws them. Write ages, diagnoses and test results under the symbol with Notes.

Your data

Pedigree Studio runs entirely in your browser and makes no network requests: nothing you draw is sent anywhere. The pedigree you are working on is kept in this browser's own storage so that it is still here next time; Clear the canvas under File removes it. Exported pictures and pedigree files are made on this device.

How Tidy works

Tidy treats the pedigree as a layered drawing, the approach introduced by Sugiyama et al. (1981). It puts each person in a generation by the network simplex method of Gansner et al. (1993). It orders each row to keep families together with as few crossing lines as possible, using the barycentre method of Sugiyama et al. and sifting (Matuszewski et al., 1999). It then finds the positions that centre children under their parents and keep lines straight, by solving a small optimisation problem with FISTA (Beck and Teboulle, 2009), using the adaptive restarts of O'Donoghue and Candès (2015).
